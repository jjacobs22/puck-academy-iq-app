Breakout Routes

The High Route Problem

The Situation:

End of the first period, your team is defending. D-man gets the puck behind the net. You swing back to support but take a high route near the top of the circles.

Your D-man hits you with a pass, but it comes to your backhand. What went wrong?

A) The D-man made a bad pass
B) You should have stopped and called for it
C) Your route was too high — coming underneath would've put the puck on your forehand ✓ CORRECT
D) Nothing — backhand passes are just part of hockey

Why This Matters:

This isn't about the pass. It's about your route. A deeper, lower route puts you in a position to receive the puck with vision and control. When you're high, you're often catching it on your backhand with pressure already coming. The bobble and the 2-on-0 against? That started with route selection.

Tyler-Specific Note:

"This is one small adjustment that changes everything. Get lower, come underneath, and you'll have time to see the whole ice."

Faceoff Mechanics

Faceoff Leverage

The Situation:

Defensive zone draw. You set up in your usual stance — low on the stick, but legs fairly straight. The opposing center is coiled and ready.

What adjustment gives you a better chance?

A) Grip lower on the stick for more control
B) Get lower through the hips and knees — load your legs ✓ CORRECT
C) Watch the opposing center's eyes for a tell
D) Lean in more with your upper body

Why This Matters:

Leverage comes from the ground up. Straight legs mean you're pushing with your arms, not your whole body. Getting lower through hips and knees lets you explode into the draw. It's not about being stronger than the other guy — it's about being in a position to use the strength you have.

Tyler-Specific Note:

"I've seen you go low on the stick, which is good. Now match that with your legs. Get loaded. You'll feel the difference immediately."

Backcheck Awareness

The Situation:

The other team breaks out quickly. You're backchecking hard but you've got your head down, focused on getting back. A forward sneaks in behind you as the late trailer.

A) Skating harder — speed is the issue
B) Shoulder-checking constantly while backchecking ✓ CORRECT
C) Yelling at your D-man to pick him up
D) Staying higher in the zone to prevent the breakout

Why This Matters:

Backchecking isn't just skating hard. It's scanning. Shoulder-check every few strides. Know where the late man is. Your job is to identify the threat and take it away — not just get back to the zone. The late trailer beats you when you don't know he's there.

Tyler-Specific Note:

"You're doing the work to get back. Now add the awareness. Head on a swivel. Find the dangerous guy before he finds the puck."
