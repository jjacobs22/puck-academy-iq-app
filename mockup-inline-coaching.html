Coach Cues Across All 6 Modules

Scenario 2-1: Reading the Ref Position

Module 2: Faceoffs
🏒
Winning the draw starts before the puck drops. Watch the linesman's body — where they're leaning tells you where to attack.
Current situation text:
"Defensive zone faceoff to goalie's left. You're a left-handed shot. Linesman angled slightly toward YOUR side. Opposing center is a righty with strong backhand grip."
Proposed situation text:
"Defensive zone faceoff. You're a lefty — forehand is to your right. The linesman is angled slightly toward your side, which means he'll likely drop the puck closer to you. The opposing center is a righty who favors his backhand. How do you attack this draw?"

Scenario 3-1: High Route vs. Low Route

Module 3: Breakouts
🏒
A breakout isn't a race — it's about the right route. Where you receive the puck changes everything about what happens next.
Current situation text:
"Your D-man retrieves puck behind net. One forechecker pressuring hard. You swinging back from far side to support."
Proposed situation text:
"Your D has the puck behind the net with a forechecker closing hard. You're swinging back from the far side to give him an option. You can come high near the blue line or go low below the hash marks. Think about how you'll receive the puck — forehand or backhand? — and what happens next."

Scenario 4-1: Net Front Presence

Module 4: Offensive Zone
🏒
The net front is the hardest area on the ice — and the most rewarding. When the puck is down low, that's where centers score greasy goals.
Current situation text:
"Your winger has puck on half wall below circle. Your D at point. Opposing D in slot. You at high slot."
Proposed situation text:
"Your winger has the puck on the half wall below the circle. Your D is at the point. The opposing D is in front of you. You're hanging out at the high slot — safe, but not dangerous. The puck is down low. Where should you be?"

Scenario 5-1: F1 Angle of Approach

Module 5: Forechecking
🏒
F1 doesn't chase — you take an angle that limits where the D can go. Forechecking is about angles, not straight lines.
Current situation text:
"You're F1 entering zone on forecheck. D has puck behind net. Their other D on far side. F2 trailing on weak side."
Proposed situation text:
"You're first in on the forecheck. The D has the puck behind his net, and his partner is on the far side — that D-to-D pass is his best option. Your F2 is trailing on the weak side. What angle do you take to dictate where this puck goes?"

Scenario 6-1: Gap Control

Module 6: D-Zone (Defensemen)
🏒
Gap is everything. Too far and they walk around you. Too tight and they blow by. The right gap depends on speed, support, and where you are on the ice.
Current situation text:
"2-on-2 rush coming. Forward has puck in neutral zone with speed. Your partner covering trailer. You at own blue line."
Proposed situation text:
"A 2-on-2 rush is coming at you. The puck carrier has speed through the neutral zone and he's not slowing down. Your partner has the trailer. You're standing at your blue line — how much ice are you going to give him?"

What Happens to the 3-Slide Intros?

Design Decision

Don't delete them. The content is good — it just shouldn't be a gate.

Move them to an optional "Learn the Basics" link at the module level on the training hub. Players who want the primer can tap it. Players who want to jump in can jump in.

You already have "Review intro" links — this just makes that the default path instead of the forced overlay.

Exception: Module 2 (Faceoffs) might keep a single-slide primer about forehand/backhand since that's genuinely unfamiliar terminology for many players. One slide, not three.
