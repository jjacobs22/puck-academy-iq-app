## Areas to Develop

### 🎯 PRIMARY FOCUS: Breakout Support Timing

Tyler tends to stop at the hash marks and wait for passes on breakouts. This makes him a stationary target that forecheckers can easily take away. In Shifts 5, 10, and 12, the D had the puck behind the net while Tyler was standing still rather than skating through the zone.

**What to work on:** Breakout timing is about "skating to where you'll receive the puck, not standing where you are now." Your route should be in motion *before* the pass, not after. Start moving earlier — this pulls defenders with you and opens up passing lanes for your D.

> **Coach's Note:** The fix here is simple: feet moving earlier. Every second you're skating on a breakout, you're gaining an advantage. Every second you're standing, the defense is recovering. The best centermen are never still in the D-zone. Give your D a moving target.
